## Pitch (draft, ~180 words)

In May I published data showing lawyer-coders don't work on each other's code — and admitted my last merged PR into someone else's legal-OSS repo was a docs typo from 2019. At the end of June, I finally crossed my own line: I started helping maintain lq-ai, the LegalQuants community project. `[EMOTIONAL CORE — Houfu's specific moment goes here; do not invent]`. This is the field report Part 1 promised: what maintaining code you didn't write actually asks of you, why the author skills I built on redlines didn't transfer the way I expected, and the tooling I tried to build (lq-ai-maintainer) to support the work. It ends with a confession about ambition. I used to want to be the person with the great idea, given away for free. One month in, I think the longer future — mine, and maybe legal open source's — is in the unglamorous middle: reviewing, mentoring, and guiding projects (perhaps even law firms') to sustainability. Neither dream is wrong. But only one of them scales past a single author.
