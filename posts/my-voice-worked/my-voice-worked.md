This is a subscriber's only newsletter. There's a lot of ground to cover (Claude's new Legal Agent?! Moltbook?! Legal Quants!?), so I would like to get this personal post out of the way first. My blog (coding) agent seem to do poorly at personal posts, so (surprise, surprise) 100% of this has to be done by hand.

To be honest, I did have a goal to do some public speaking in 2026. When I restarted this newsletter in the form of alt-counsel, I wanted to only do 1 detailed post a month due to time constraints. As the record goes, [with big help from Claude Code,](https://github.com/houfu/blog-alt-counsel) I over-performed. Simply speaking, as I managed to develop my ideas more fully and put them in writing, getting people to see value in it became much easier.

<!--members-only-->

So while I was genuinely surprised to hear from Cheryl (an alt-counsel subscriber!) ask me whether I wanted to speak at Drew & Napier's seminar, I jumped on and dived straight into it. By all accounts, it was a big success to me personally, and I am really grateful.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/33/4e/334edc26-d66c-4112-a0d8-6528c3cd17c4/content/images/2026/02/image.png)

Getting to stand/sit in front of a public audience was an interesting experience. One of my biggest fears was that having written so much, and relied so much on AI for that, will the human being be able to convey it? During our lunch meetings, Cheryl was very supporting and reassuring. It felt to me though, like I was studying for an exam.

And like many exams, things happen. I brought my red notebook up to take notes if I had to. But I left my flash cards in my bag. I had to rely on my fleshy mushy brain.

Here's another thing I learnt about myself while prepping for this opportunity. I wondered sometimes why I stalled and hesitated at times to answer. It turns out I was trying to reach for the "proper" answer, but not the answer I believed in. By being genuine, I was more engaged and more convincing. Others can see it when my face lit up.

So in my moment of panic, I told myself to stick to my genuinely held beliefs and let it go. The words flowed, I had fun and I felt that the audience took something away from spending time thinking about AI.

As a debut, this might be the longest time I will spend writing about public speaking. These are very interesting times we live in. I have lots of other things to write about, and hopefully more places where I can talk to them to other lawyers.

And the truth is, AI is a big part of why this happened.

If you felt that there's nothing much you can take from alt-counsel (whether because you don't really code, don't really law or don't have enough time to do either of those), I hope I've convinced you that AI can change your life for the better.

As long as you use your real voice.
