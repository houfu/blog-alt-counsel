## What this gives the LawNet post

Two things, and the first is significant.

**1. Slide 14 is independent evidence, presented at TechLaw.Fest itself.** "A number of interviewees had
never heard of or accessed LawNet.com and LawNet AI." A year after the 4.0 launch, from research
presented at the same conference. That is not Houfu's opinion that the strategy isn't landing — it is a
finding someone else brought to SAL's own stage. It also reframes the proposed fix as *communications*
(demos, how-to guides, incentives), which is itself telling: the instinct is to market the bookcase
harder rather than ask what is on the shelves.

**2. Slides 18 and 19 are a capability roadmap someone else wrote for LawNet.** Review/verify first,
recommend edits next, generate first draft as the long-term goal — plus a citation checker and a
contract-review tool built on LawNet's existing Precedents function. This is exactly the "three exits"
argument arriving from the floor rather than from the podium. Useful as corroboration that Houfu is not
alone in seeing the gap, and it lets the post be constructive without becoming a demand that LawNet
become CoCounsel.

⚠️ **Careful with the survey numbers.** Sample size, population and methodology are not in these
photos. The counts on slides 6, 7 and 12 look like a survey of roughly 100–120 respondents, but that is
an inference from the axis, not a fact. Do not print a percentage derived from these counts without the
underlying study.

## What this gives the juniors post (candidate #2)

Slide 17 is close to a ready-made spine for it, and it complicates the thesis in a useful way. The
pastoral-vs-operational framing said nobody owns the question of how juniors acquire judgment when the
judgment-building work is automated. Slide 17 shows someone *does* own it, at least as a proposal: a
3-stage framework, a training audit questionnaire and a living compendium. That is a real answer worth
engaging with rather than a gap to point at. Whether a questionnaire and a compendium are adequate to
the problem is the interesting question, and it is a better post than "nobody is thinking about this."
