## 2. Takeaway 2 — LawNet: the evidence is on your side

### What LawNet announced AT TechLaw.Fest 2026

From [Justice Kwek Mean Luck's Day 2 speech](https://sal.org.sg/articles/speech-by-the-honourable-justice-kewk-mean-luck-at-techlaw-fest-2026):

1. AI Q&A expanded from **contract law only → all areas of Singapore law** (scope, not capability)
2. **Academy Publishing books** added to both Classic Search and AI Q&A
3. Legislation coverage extended to **ten key statutes** (incl. Penal Code, Evidence Act), with cases tagged to each provision
4. **AI-generated annotations** — extracted legal principles per statutory provision
5. Partnership with **LegalOn Technologies** to push corporate legal content into external platforms
6. Revamped **Sentencing Information and Research Repository**, launching next year

**Not one of the six is a workflow, drafting, or agentic capability.** Five of six are corpus expansion or corpus organisation. This is the single strongest piece of evidence for the "bigger bookcase" thesis, and it comes from SAL's own podium.

### 🔑 The sharpest verifiable detail: the locked shelf

From the Sep 2026 release notes, on books now feeding AI Search:

> "Please note that access to the full book content requires a valid purchase of the digital book or an Academy Library subscription."

**The AI now grounds answers in sources the user may not be able to open.** You can see the extract that generated the answer; reading the book costs more. That is a bigger bookcase where some shelves are locked — and it is an A2J point with teeth because it is LawNet's own documentation, not an opinion.

### The counter-case (for the "neither is wrong" move)

LawNet's strategy is *defensible*, and the draft is stronger if it says so before dissenting:

- **Trust & safety engineering the global vendors don't show as visibly.** AI summaries flag low-confidence paragraphs in **yellow**, terms not in the source document in **red**, and give paragraph footnotes cross-referencing summary to source. (Dec 2025 release notes)
- **Search Logs** exist precisely because "LawNet AI may produce different responses to similar queries" — an honest admission of non-determinism, with an audit trail.
- Authority and verifiability are a legitimate thing to optimise for in a **national** legal information utility during a bicentenary.
- LawNet AI Search is **included in the basic subscription** — no separate AI upsell. ([Ask.Legal comparison, Aug 2026](https://ask.legal/sg/blog/ask-legal-ai-vs-lawnet-westlaw-asia-2026-comparison))

The honest framing is therefore **not** "LawNet is incompetent." It is: **LawNet optimised for verifiability; the global vendors optimised for doing the work.** Neither is wrong. But only one of those bets compounds, and the A2J question is whether a verifiable bookcase behind a paywall helps the people who most need help.

## 3. Takeaway 1 — juniors: the diagnosis needs refining

### The complication ⚠️

The pitch says the danger is that "focus remains on personal productivity, ROI and security/harm prevention" while juniors get left behind. But Singapore's institutions have spent 2026 talking about juniors **constantly**:

- **12 Jan 2026, Opening of Legal Year:** CJ Menon and Law Minister Edwin Tong announced they will **co-lead a committee** on lawyer attrition and the impact of AI. ([Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/courts-crime/law-minister-and-chief-justice-to-co-lead-committee-to-tackle-lawyer-attrition-rates-impact-of-ai))
- **20 Apr 2026, Mass Call:** CJ Menon's address was literally titled *"A Profession in Transition — Preparing and Supporting the Next Generation."* Reported figures: **1 in 3 new lawyers may quit within 3 years** (workload, culture) and **92% of new lawyers were already using AI in their work**. ([CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/2026-mass-call-high-court-chief-justice-ai-work-practices-6067356))
- **29 Jul 2026, Legal Profession Symposium:** CJ Menon on sustainable legal careers, naming billable-hour targets as a source of stress for young lawyers. ([SAL](https://sal.org.sg/articles/speech-by-chief-justice-sundaresh-menon-at-the-legal-profession-symposium-2026), [HRO](https://www.humanresourcesonline.net/chief-justice-sundaresh-menon-calls-for-deeper-focus-on-sustainable-legal-careers-as-ai-reshapes-the-profession))
- SAL runs a **Junior Lawyers Professional Certification Programme**.

**So "nobody is thinking about juniors" is rebuttable in one sentence, and a reviewer will rebut it.**

### The defensible version of the claim

The institutional conversation about juniors (attrition, culture, billable hours, wellbeing) runs on a **completely separate track** from the adoption conversation on the conference floor (personal productivity, ROI, security and harm prevention). Nothing connects them. Nobody at the vendor booths is selling "this is how your second-year learns judgment." The juniors discourse is **pastoral** — retention, wellbeing, culture — while the AI discourse is **operational**. The gap is not attention. The gap is that **no one owns the question of how a junior acquires judgment when the work that used to build it is the work being automated.**

That version survives contact with the CJ's speeches. It is also more interesting.

### Supporting external evidence

- **Axios, 2 May 2026:** "AI is wiping out some entry-level work that trains the next generation of elite lawyers." ([link](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/02/ai-lawyers-law-firms-artificial-intelligence))
- **Chambers, 18 May 2026:** relying on AI to replace junior work "may weaken the future partnership pipeline by limiting early-career skill development." ([link](https://chambers.com/topics/law-firm-succession-planning-ai-lawyers))
- **Thomson Reuters Institute, 2 Feb 2026:** counter-view — "When people say 'AI will eliminate junior training,' they're making a category error," confusing specific tasks with the training function. ([link](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/articles/ai-law-professor-train-junior-lawyers)) — **useful for the "neither is wrong" move.**
- **IBA, 16 Mar 2026:** training identified as key priority, both teaching staff about AI and coaching associates. ([link](https://www.ibanet.org/ai-technology-presents-both-opportunity-and-threat-to-young-lawyers))
- **ACC/Everlaw survey (Sep 2026):** 52% of in-house teams use AI tools, **only 7% have seen cost reductions** — good ammunition against the ROI framing.
- **LexisNexis APAC AI Sentiment Survey 2026:** 94% of APAC legal professionals use AI; only **58% work under an AI policy**. ([link](https://www.lexisnexis.com/community/au-resources/b/whitepapers/posts/2026-apac-ai-sentiment-survey-report-adoption-is-near-universal-governance-is-not))
- **MOM, 30 Apr 2026:** among SG firms using AI, 70.7% report productivity improvements. ([link](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2026/0430-adoption-of-ai-among-firms))
