## Pitch

In January I made five testable predictions about legal AI and promised a December scorecard, with $500 to legal aid if I chickened out. In July I confessed my confidence was inverted. By December even that lesson hadn't held. The jagged frontier, July's story of the year, faded as models — local ones like Qwen 3.8 included — got predictable enough to make writing your own evals worthwhile. The hallucination problem I'd turned pessimistic on became far more manageable once we got better at context. Contract review without Word is a plain fail: not because agents couldn't do it, but because Word is where I read. My AI usage hovered at 50–66%. Theatre held, and ASEAN regulated rather than leapfrogged. The pattern across the year: AI work drifts to whoever holds the context — business users running an agent in their own Copilot, lawyers feeding models what they need. My best evidence of real innovation wasn't a prediction at all, but Marshall, a Singapore-law agent I assembled from other lawyers' open-source parts. For solo counsel, that's the hopeful bit: you can build without waiting to be given access.
